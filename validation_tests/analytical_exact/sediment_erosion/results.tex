
\section{Entrainment of bed sediment into still water}

A tank of still water 1~m deep stands over a fixed plane bed of slope
$S = 10^{-3}$. The sediment is 0.5~mm sand with the settling velocity $v_s$
of Ferguson and Church~\cite{FergusonChurch2004}. There is no flow, so the
case uses the depth--slope closure, under which the bed shear stress of a
cell depends only on its depth and bed slope, $\tau_b = \rho g h S$, and the
Shields stress is $\tau^{*} = hS/(RD) \approx 1.2$ with $R = \rho_s/\rho - 1$.
That is some $30\,\tau_c^{*}$ for the sand ($\tau_c^{*} = 0.04$), and the
non-cohesive entrainment law of Smith and McLean fires,
\begin{equation}
E = v_s E^{*}, \qquad
E^{*} = \frac{0.65\,\gamma_0 X}{1 + \gamma_0 X}, \qquad
X = \frac{\tau^{*}}{\tau_c^{*}} - 1, \qquad \gamma_0 = 0.0024 ,
\end{equation}
while deposition returns sediment to the bed at the well-mixed rate
$D = d^{*} v_s c$ with $d^{*} = 1$. A second fraction of the same grain but
$\tau_c^{*} = 10$ lies below its threshold and must stay at zero: the
control. Every cell is a closed system, so the sediment mass per unit area
$m = hc$ obeys $dm/dt = E - D$, and from $c = 0$ the concentration relaxes
to the equilibrium value $c_{eq} = E/(d^{*}v_s)$ along
\begin{equation}
c(t) = c_{eq}\left(1 - e^{-t/T}\right), \qquad T = \frac{h}{d^{*} v_s} \approx 10~\mathrm{s} ,
\end{equation}
each cell on its own curve since $h$ varies along the slope. The bed is
held fixed on purpose: the depth--slope closure takes $S$ from the bed
itself, so once the bed evolves any non-uniform erosion steepens local
slopes, raises $\tau_b$ and erodes faster, and the case has no closed-form
reference. That is the closure's own property; the bed update is checked
by the settling case. The case checks the entrainment law and its
threshold, the depth--slope closure in every cell (the slope estimate must
reproduce the plane along the walls and in the corners as well as in the
interior), and the balance of entrainment against deposition in the
conserved sediment mass.
